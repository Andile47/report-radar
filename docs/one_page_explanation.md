# Report Radar: what it found, in plain language

*For municipal managers and other non-technical readers. All figures come from a year of synthetic (realistic but fake) reports for Alexandra, Johannesburg, from 22 October 2025 to 21 October 2026.*

## The problem

Residents report burst pipes, potholes, power faults, sewage spills, uncollected rubbish and broken streetlights. Every report has to be read, sorted, prioritised and sent to the right team. When reports suddenly surge, for example in a heatwave or after a storm, teams fall behind and urgent problems wait.

## What Report Radar does

1. **Sorts each report automatically.** It reads what the resident wrote and decides the type of problem and how urgent it is, from 1 (cosmetic) to 5 (danger to life). It understands typos and common isiZulu phrases. When it isn't sure, it marks the report for a person to check instead of guessing.
2. **Forecasts the workload.** From past weeks it predicts how many reports will arrive over the next 4 weeks.
3. **Maps the hotspots.** It groups reports by location and ranks the areas from busiest to quietest.

## What we found

- **About 69 reports a week are expected for the next month (276 in total, likely between 260 and 292).** That is the same level as the last 4 weeks, so no surge is expected and current staffing should cope.
- **Water is the problem to watch.** It is the only service with a rising trend, about 1 extra report a week (+8%). It is also the most common problem in every hotspot, and it surged before: the busiest week of the year (12 January 2026, 132 reports, nearly double a normal week) was a summer water crisis.
- **Two areas generate most of the work.** Old Alexandra and East Bank together account for 61% of all reports. Placing field teams and depots near them would cut response times the most.
- **Urgency is similar everywhere** (an average of about 3 out of 5 in every hotspot). The busy areas need more capacity, not different priorities.

## How much to trust it

- The forecast is usually within **about 4 reports a week** of what actually happened when we tested it on past weeks (about 5% of a normal week).
- The report sorter puts reports in the right category in 26 out of 30 deliberately tricky test reports. The mistakes were unusual wording, e.g. "dead dog on the pavement" wasn't recognised as a refuse problem. Unclear reports go to a person rather than being guessed.

## What it can't do (yet)

- **It has only one year of history**, so it can't yet learn yearly patterns such as the January water peak. Next summer's surge would not be predicted in advance; it would show up quickly once it starts.
- **Fewer reports doesn't mean fewer problems.** Some areas may simply report less, for example because residents have less airtime or less trust that reports are acted on. Tsutsumani reports the least; the Week 6 fairness check should look at whether it is under-served.
- **The data is synthetic.** The methods work, but the findings must be re-checked on real reports before decisions are made.

## What we recommend

1. Use the 4-week forecast in weekly planning meetings, and act on the dashboard's surge alert when it appears.
2. Base extra water-maintenance capacity in or near Old Alexandra and East Bank, especially before summer.
3. Have a person check the reports marked "needs review" each day.
4. Before using Report Radar for real decisions, test it on a sample of real reports.
